# Code of Conduct

MusicLab exists so more people can feel the joy of making music that comes from them — especially people who felt shut out of traditional instruments, music notation, or complex audio software.

Everyone who participates in this repository — whether sharing feedback from a play session, asking a beginner question, adapting MusicLab for a classroom or switch setup, or writing code — is expected to keep this space welcoming, patient, and respectful.

## What we ask of everyone

1. **Respect disabled players, beginners, and supporters.** Never mock someone's musical taste, assistive technology, access needs, or technical experience. Saying *"I don't consider myself musical"* or *"I've never used GitHub before"* is explicitly welcome here.
2. **Protect privacy when sharing play stories.** When describing how MusicLab worked for a child, student, client, or family member, **do not post real names, photos, recordings of voices, or medical diagnoses**. Describe only the setup that matters (for example: *"playing with two switches mapped to Space and Enter on a laptop"*).
3. **Keep language plain and constructive.** Critique ideas and interfaces, not people. Avoid gatekeeping jargon when simpler words work.
4. **Honour player authorship and consent.** Do not propose dark patterns, autoplay audio, hidden tracking, countdown pressure, or features that silently replace a person's musical choices and call the result theirs.

## Unacceptable behavior

- Ableism, harassment, insults, or demeaning comments about disability, neurodivergence, age, gender, race, religion, or skill level.
- Sharing another person's private or identifying information without consent.
- Trolling, spam, or derailing discussions in Issues or Pull Requests.

## Reporting a problem

If someone's behavior in this repository makes it unsafe or unwelcoming, you can report it to the maintainer through GitHub (including privately via the repository's **Security → Report a vulnerability** channel if no public issue is appropriate). Maintainers may edit, hide, or remove comments, issues, or pull requests that break these guidelines.
